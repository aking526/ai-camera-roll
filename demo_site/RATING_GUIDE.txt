A LIFE IN FRAMES - HUMAN RATING GUIDE
Local development round for CIS 5810

OPEN THE REVIEW SECTION
Keep Open_Demo.command running, then open:
http://127.0.0.1:5812/#ratings
Select Rate photos in the persistent navigation: Photos / Pipeline / Rate photos.

This section reads saved images and saves human answers. It does not call the
story or image-generation providers, so an expired generation key does not
prevent rating. The local address works on this Mac; it is not a remote link
that teammates can open on their own computers.

ONE ROUND, THREE INDEPENDENT REVIEWS
A round is a fixed image set and rubric. The first round contains Maya's five
FINAL photographs, not all nine intermediate passes. Each actual teammate
contributes one review of that same set using their own name or initials.
Each reviewer gets a shuffled order that remains stable when they resume.
Model scores, previous review notes and generation-method labels are hidden in
the rating view. Maya is still familiar development material, not an unseen
or fully blinded benchmark. Record prior exposure honestly.

ADD YOUR FIRST REVIEW
1. Select Maya's original album in Photos, then open Rate photos. Choose
   "Start my review" on Maya's card, or "Continue my review" for a saved draft.
   For any album with no round, including a fresh checkout, "Set up a rating round" freezes its final
   photos and references without generating new images.
2. Enter your own name or initials. Answer whether you have already seen these
   photographs, their review notes or their scores. Check the independence
   statement, then choose "Begin my review".
3. Read "How to rate" before the first photograph. Compare what is actually
   visible with the canonical portrait, object references and requested scene.
4. Follow People -> Objects -> Scene for each photo. Each section shows only
   the relevant questions. Use Next to continue, or the section tabs to go back.
   Optional observations in Scene can explain a defect or uncertainty.
5. Drafts save as you answer. Use "Save & exit" to pause. Wait for "Saved locally" before closing or
   reloading. If saving fails, keep the page open and restore the local server;
   do not assume an unsaved answer reached disk.
6. After Scene, choose "Next photo". Repeat for all five photographs, then
   choose "Check my answers". Review the summary and use "Edit photograph"
   to correct a draft answer if needed. When all photos are complete, the
   Scene step returns directly to the summary.
7. Choose "Submit my 5 ratings" only when your review is complete. Submission
   locks the answers so the original observations remain intact. You can then
   select "Download my ratings" to keep your own exported copy.

HOW TO MAKE EACH JUDGMENT
Identity: compare the visible face with the protagonist's portrait.
- Same person: facial features are consistent, allowing changes in expression,
  viewing angle and appearance.
- Different: a visible likely protagonist has clearly different features.
- Unclear: the face is too small, obscured or ambiguous to judge confidently.
- No identifiable candidate: you cannot locate a plausible protagonist.
A hat, clothes, activity or position in the scene does not establish identity.
Do not turn the lack of a confident comparison into a confident mismatch.

Visible people: count people independently of who they are. If overlapping or
ambiguous figures prevent a confident count, check that option rather than
entering the number requested by the prompt.

Required objects: evaluate each listed object separately.
- Presence: Present, Absent or Unclear, based on a recognizable visible candidate.
- Count: for a present object, count visible instances of the required type;
  extras count as duplicates.
- Appearance: compare its visible design with the canonical reference. Choose
  Matches reference, Different, or Unclear.
- State: compare placement, ownership and condition with the requested scene.
  Correct meets the request; Partly meets some but not all details; Wrong
  contradicts the request; Unclear lacks enough visible evidence.
Absent automatically means zero instances and no appearance/state judgment.
Unclear presence leaves those other judgments unassessed. These derived
not-applicable values are not separate judgments that the object is correct.

Setting and main action: judge these separately with Yes, Partly, No or Unclear.
Partly means some requested elements are satisfied. Unclear means you cannot
tell from the image. A convincing-looking photograph can still miss its request.

RESUME AND FINISH THE TEAM ROUND
Use Continue my review when shown. Otherwise choose Start my review (or
Review as someone else) and enter your original name. The same name resumes that review; it does not add another vote.
Do not submit under several names or rate on another person's behalf.

Let each of the three real reviewers finish independently on this Mac. Avoid
reading one another's responses or the existing evaluation while rating.
Team results unlock after three reviewers submit. With three reviewers, a
strict majority means at least two agree. Unclear cases and unresolved votes
remain visible in the results; one submitted review is not a team majority.
After the team finishes, use "View team results" and "Download round data".
The three-iteration plan remains under the collapsed Evaluation plan section.

KEEP THE EVIDENCE
Drafts, submissions and frozen assets are saved under private/ratings/.
Exports contain rating data and round information, not provider API keys.
The round export preserves raw submitted ratings, the rubric, source mapping
and image hashes. Keep it with the experiment record. Do not replace original
ratings with a group-agreed answer after discussion; add interpretations in
separate analysis instead. Reviewer names and observations are part of the
export, so review the file before sharing it.

USING THIS WORK TO START ITERATION 1
Building the rating workflow starts BASELINE MEASUREMENT. It does not by itself
complete an evaluation-driven development iteration or demonstrate improvement.

Complete iteration 1 as a short evidence chain:
1. Collect the three independent reviews of the frozen Maya baseline.
2. Identify one repeated defect supported by those judgments, while inspecting
   disagreements and unclear cases. Write one testable hypothesis.
3. Change one relevant part of the generation pipeline. Keep the scene
   requirements and other comparison conditions paired, and record all calls.
4. Save the revised outputs as a new album, start a new frozen rating round,
   and use the same rubric. Rate independently again without viewing scores.
5. Compare identity, required objects, setting/action and generation cost.
   Record improvements, regressions and uncertainty. Decide whether to keep
   or reject the change. A failed hypothesis is still useful evidence.

For the report, preserve: hypothesis; baseline round ID; exact change; revised
round ID; comparison results; generation calls/runtime; and the final decision.
The two rounds are a development comparison, not proof of broad generalization.
Live generation must be available before generating revised outputs.

THREE ITERATIONS: A FOCUSED PLAN
Iteration 1: use these ratings to choose and test one specific consistency fix.
Iteration 2: calibrate a face-score trigger on development labels, then compare
            the same initial outputs with and without one bounded retry. If the
            scores cannot support a reliable trigger, record that finding first.
Iteration 3: use the remaining failures to choose one further focused change,
            such as face preservation during edits or object-state instructions,
            then run another paired human evaluation.

These are planned cycles beyond the proposal, not three completed iterations.
Each needs an actual change and comparable before/after evidence. Keep the
rubric consistent; if it must change, record its version and explain which
results are still comparable. Confirm the planned scope with the TA.

LOCAL DATA
Review records and frozen assets are stored in demo_site/private/ratings/,
which is excluded from Git. Keep this folder to preserve your reviews when
updating the website. The repository ships no actual or artificial reviewer
submissions. Code changes can be reverted independently of these local records.
